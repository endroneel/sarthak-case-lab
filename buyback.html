Two ways to share leftover-inventory risk

The corporate plan is valuable only when its quantity, price, reliability, quality conditions and payment terms are explicit. The calculations below treat the contract as a decision-support model, not as a substitute for managerial judgment.

Plan A

Guaranteed capped buyback

The partner purchases at most B eligible leftover units at the corporate price. Any leftover inventory above the cap receives the ordinary clearance value.

Clearance revenue = vb min(L,B) + vo max(L−B,0)
Plan B

Probabilistic commitment

The partner accepts all eligible leftovers with probability ρ. Otherwise, the leftovers are sold through the ordinary clearance channel.

veffective = ρvb + (1−ρ)vo

Contract conditions to verify

  • Is the cap a firm obligation or only an expression of interest?
  • Is ₹58.60 the net amount after transport, packaging, quality checking and commissions?
  • Which products qualify, and who determines whether quality standards are met?
  • When does the partner confirm acceptance, take delivery and make payment?
  • What happens to leftovers above the cap or after a rejected probabilistic commitment?

Managerial interpretation

A capped plan protects SARTHAK only up to a known quantity. A probabilistic plan protects all leftovers only in expectation and transfers reliability risk back to SARTHAK.

Modelling caution: the normal-demand assumption, estimated cost, salvage values and commitment probability are managerial approximations. The contract should be piloted and recalibrated using realized sales, rejection rates, payment delays and actual net recovery.
